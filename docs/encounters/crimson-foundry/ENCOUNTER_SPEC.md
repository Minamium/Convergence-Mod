# Scarlet Invocation

This specification supersedes the simultaneous-three-apparition prototype and the flat physical renderer. [Status](../../STATUS.md) owns the current build and verification; implementation does not imply visual playtest approval.

**Vespera — The Scarlet Conjurer** conducts three independent apparitions. The player-facing Raid and item names are **Scarlet Invocation** and **Scarlet Grimoire**; stable `crimson_foundry`, CrimsonFoundry code, packet, save and asset IDs remain unchanged. The companion is Scarlet Covenant. Vespera remains ordinary NPC scale; only her apparitions are large.

## Party, preparation and authority

Use Scarlet Grimoire on the existing Foundation Core. Theater Doll still selects the separate Doll Raid. Server-held-item/range/world/lease validation, server-wide 1–8-player preparation, manual Ready and a frozen roster with connection tokens remain. A roster change resets Ready. No fake participants, autonomous companion substitution, new world generation or automatic rejoin is added. Falling in combat uses [Down and instant recovery](#down-and-instant-recovery), not ordinary death. The 160×70-tile grounded field, containment/lift and natural-spawn suppression remain.

The Doll attendant is only visible when the exact Doll Encounter/Fight/preparation and pedestal position match. A generic protected pedestal does not summon her. Scarlet spawn, AI and draw do not acquire ownership of the Doll preparation actor.

Ready uses the same minimal fixed top-center button as Doll and Cathedral: physical viewport center,200×36px at y64, on an unscaled interface layer. Click bounds match drawing. Small world-space `Ready!` labels appear above all ready participants, including the local player; UI scale does not reposition the button or double-transform those labels.

### Orb-to-invocation opening

Preparing shows only the floating red energy body, Ready/field containment and the normal world; neither Vespera nor the cathedral appears yet. All-Ready starts a protected opening of one lead second plus the intro's eight bars (16 seconds): the orb unravels into the56px Vespera silhouette, then etched counter-rotating seals grow above her; the cathedral fades in only after her materialization. Ember Crown emerges at the shared summoning gate on the fourth bar (about8.5seconds after All-Ready), with an owned rupture cue/recoil. A longer designation and optional camera pan connect these events before combat unlocks. Input is not disabled; Reduced Effects/camera-off preserve timing and forecasts without forced camera motion. State/timing live in `CrimsonChoreography`, not independent client timers.

Vespera's native center remains fixed at the field center in every Act, including Final. Apparitions alone stage/move. Grounded containment uses the actual support surface, not a2px raised plane. Owner prediction handles ordinary edge contact; server jitter correction does not repeatedly emit native teleport sounds. Only corrections exceeding48px send a rate-limited teleport notification, logged as `MajorFieldCorrection`; large escape correction remains functional.

## Acts, health and complete action cycles

| Stage | Target | Exit |
|---|---|---|
| Introduction | Vespera, then Ember Crown | Accepted score introduction finishes |
| Act I | Ember Crown | Hold at20% HP and finish the current full action cycle |
| Act II | Sable Mantle | Hold at20% and finish the current full action cycle |
| Act III | Thorn Choir | Hold at20% and finish the current full action cycle |
| Final | Scarlet Eidolon, one giant apparition formed by sacrificing all three | Complete its first ensemble cycle, then defeat the single giant |

An action cycle is **12 complete attack phrases**, using the eight-beat choreography below. Five phrases can be followed by a Stack/Spread chorus in Act II onward. A chorus is not counted as one of the12 phrases. Completion waits for admitted phrases' last recovery/tail and any outstanding chorus. Issuing the last phrase is not completion. At a safe cycle boundary, a latched20% threshold changes exactly one act on the next bar head of the [128 BPM grid](#music-graceful-ordeal-on-a-128-bpm-grid); otherwise another full cycle starts. No mid-phrase cancellation is used to accelerate the health transition.

Native incoming damage is capped before crossing20% in solo acts. Once reached, HP remains exactly at that floor while the apparition continues its repertoire. The same NPC and HP are retained offstage after retreat, without loot/replacement/refill. Act II/III use two protected bars (225 ticks): during the first the retiring body moves to the gate and its surface erodes into flowing red energy while the old music continues; the music's full stop or interlude sweep lands on the second bar's downbeat (`ActRelease`), and the next body reconstitutes before the next act's downbeat unlocks combat. `CrimsonEnsemble` owns these curves and deadlines.

Final uses five protected bars (562 ticks): the old music's last bar, the full stop, two riser bars and the pre-drop. Three child seals at top/lower-right/lower-left visibly re-summon the retained apparitions. Vespera's sphere settles at the center; she dissolves into it. Red energy streams emerge from all three bodies, gather into the growing sphere, and consume their surfaces. After complete convergence a shaped crimson flash on the pre-drop releases the giant; the climax's downbeat unlocks combat. Server-side sacrifice retires exactly the three owned NPCs without loot before combat unlock. **Only the giant is a Final combat target**; Vespera is visually absorbed, not a second target. Her existing native actor carries the giant's420×360 central hitbox and combined remaining HP. No fifth actor or extra HP budget is created. A large flowing red sphere is seated in its chest during combat.

The giant retains a1HP lethal floor until the **first Final action cycle** is finished. The floor then releases; no automatic death is invented. Attack-source activity remains distinct from `dontTakeDamage`, so the held giant keeps performing and the boss bar stays present.

Victory requires completed sacrifice plus the giant's defeat; the sacrifice itself is not Victory. An observed all-player wipe wins over a simultaneous clear. Unexpected missing actors invalidate the encounter, whereas the explicit sacrifice is an owned retirement. A 15-minute safety cap cancels a stalled encounter but never overrides an already committed Victory/Defeat ending. Phase changes/source deaths/teardown retire exact-Fight hazards and chorus markers; a resolved failure marker may retain its harmless48-tick visual tail through Defeat. Phase, epoch, health and completed-cycle projections reject rollback; gameplay decisions remain server/SP-owned.

### Down and instant recovery

Scarlet uses the same `Common/Raids/Revive/RaidReviveService` instant-unlimited policy as Doll, with its own exact-Fight native adapter. An accepted native lethal/floor hit makes a participant Down at1HP, held at their in-field position even in midair; Down cannot move, attack, be targeted or satisfy a chorus assignment. Merely starting at1HP does not Down. Ordinary damage still goes through Terraria; the receiving player's bounded generation-tagged floor receipt is committed by the server, never a client-authored heal or target.

Ready acceptance supplies the reusable **Resuscitation Kit** if missing. A standing ally can use it within8tiles for instant35%HP recovery and3seconds of immunity. No consumption or shared tokens. The recipient cannot be revived again for60seconds; a subsequent Down waits without an Eliminated state or timeout. All retained connected participants Down/disconnected means Defeat (also in solo). While every retained member is Down or still inside the disconnect grace, new phrases and choruses simply wait; a missing standing target never fails the fight. Success/cancel/invalidated/world exit clear the binding, locks and controls; Defeat clears the interceptor before ordinary death. God Mode is not bypassed.

Clients share the frozen slot/connection roster and monotone recovery generations. Phase changes do not reset recovery. The common feature route sends Ready/recovery and retains a full actor projection for terminal/far-player delivery. [ADR amendment](../../adr/0026-crimson-score-and-native-projectiles.md#2026-09-27-scarlet-downrecovery-and-debug-damage-protocol70) owns the native authority boundary.

## Beat choreography and Final ensemble

The active composition (protocol80, after the owner's 2026-10-03 feedback that four strikes on four consecutive beats played like a rhythm game) keeps **only the key moments exactly on bar heads** and puts sparse, syncopated notes between them. A phrase is two whole bars that always start on a bar head, on an eighth-note grid (`CrimsonMeter.EighthTick`, 14.0625 ticks). Its **ordinary notes** are three strikes, each warned exactly one measured beat earlier, never on a bar head and at least a beat apart, so at most one ordinary forecast shows at a time; the cell alternates by phrase serial: serial % 3 == 1 strikes on beats **3, 4.5, 6**, serial % 3 == 2 on **3.5, 5, 6** (Final's serial % 3 == 0 on **3, 5, 6**). The third note always strikes on beat 6, the moment the **closing crossflow's** two opposing vertical seals bloom beside one captured player position; they charge for two beats and the stream releases on **the next phrase's downbeat** (beat 8), then amplifies and collapses over two beats. **One horizontal stream from the right seal into the left seal** encloses the captured position; evade above/below it. The next phrase's first forecast waits until that stream has collapsed (beat 2), so the downbeat release has a beat to itself before the notes resume. Where no closer releases on a phrase's downbeat (an Act's first phrase, the phrase after a chorus or after an unlatched cycle boundary), the phrase opens with a **pickup crossflow** whose seals bloom two beats before its bar head and whose stream releases on it: Act I's first stream releases on the downbeat that unlocks combat, Acts II and III release theirs on the new Act's first downbeat after the transition's stop, and a chorus's last bar ends in a release. Ordinary notes carry accent 1, crossflows and a signature move's final step accent 2.

Final cycles three paired families: **tracking beam + spatial cut → spatial cut + shifted lattice → shifted lattice + tracking beam**. Each of its three ordinary notes releases both families simultaneously, each with a complete measured-beat warning (the second family carries the note's pulse + 5). **Final alone replaces the crossflow with a scarlet cluster orb**, both as closer and as pickup: a giant energy sphere grows above the Eidolon two beats before a bar head; frozen thin forecasts and sparse full-width grains announce five radial bouquets before that bar head releases them. The Final's first phrase opens with the pickup orb, so its bouquets burst on **the drop**, the climax downbeat that unlocks combat. Each bouquet contains one large, two medium and two small energy carriers. Sizes, speeds, exit bounds and continuous acceleration are owned by `CrimsonClusters`; rendering and native swept collision consume that same geometry. Directions are deterministic per phrase, never homing after warning. Gaps between bouquets admit a full player footprint; the first pair strikes at most 12 ticks before the previous orb's carriers have left (as before).

The sphere is a harmless emitter, not an additional enemy, hitbox or HP pool. Carriers shrink before reaching the field edge; trails are harmless. Their bounded96-tick flight may cross the next bar without disappearing at the old crossflow end. The next bar keeps its musical schedule; the action-cycle gate waits for the final owned flight and cleanup lease. One giant owns all seven descriptors of a phrase (eight with a pickup), with distinct pulse IDs; the25 carriers share one descriptor/native gesture, not25 new projectiles. The maximum descriptor bound remains10. Source death/epoch replacement cancels the whole owned batch. Protocol65 appends `ClusterVolley` without renumbering existing IDs; matching peers are required. The giant recombines Crown core, Mantle wings and Choir limbs with separate masks, connected fractional articulation and the existing Luminance materials, rather than enlarging a single complete PNG.

Each aimed note selects one living roster member in round-robin order, bound to their slot/connection token; crossflows and the other aimed notes rotate separately, so every roster size shares both evenly (Final's two families of one note aim at the same member). At warning onset the server takes one observation: basic attacks use Doll's18-tick velocity lead capped at220px horizontal/160px vertical; paired seals use the current position without lead. The shown forecast is immediately fixed, never dragged after presentation or retargeted to another player. Basic axes cycle vertical/horizontal/two diagonals. Replicas require the authoritative lock before showing/damaging and reject stale/different-Fight/phase/note samples. A dead/disconnected target retains its original bounded fallback. The bounded immutable chorus outcome is replicated, not client-authored damage requests. Unowned/debug-spawned native Boss/Effigy packets carry an empty-state envelope and remain harmless; owned payload validation and exact-Fight cleanup remain strict.

Act I's ordinary notes are three basic forecasts crossing **from one field boundary to the opposite boundary** through the predicted position, never starting at the Boss. Doll's managed `PortalForecastPass`, sparse `ForecastDustPass` and red/magenta `PortalBeam` mouth remain the forecast foundation; the live strike and its residue use `ScarletInk` (see Luminance presentation v2). Shared geometry owns visible width/reach and native collision; zero-width ignition and residue are harmless.

**Act II replaces the ordinary notes with SpatialRift slashes**, not alternating tracking-beam phrases (except its [signature phrases](#signature-moves-protocol79)). Each freezes one predicted player line, gives the measured beat's white space-tear warning and releases a fast red travelling cut. **Act III uses ChoirRakes** (except its signature phrases): three parallel bowed/hooked cuts per note, entering from top/right/bottom/left by the note's pulse (the two cells cover all four edges). Their full curves are forecast for a complete measured beat; rapid extension and width taper share `CrimsonChoirRakes` with native swept collision. Spacing, modest deterministic phrase offset, radius, edge inset and the96-capsule bound live there, not in local RNG. The wide passages accommodate a player footprint. Every ordinary Act I–III phrase closes with the crossflow; a signature phrase's final step takes its place. Final's paired tracking/slash/shifted-lattice families and cluster closing are unchanged. Protocol68 appends `ChoirRakes=16`; existing technique/packet IDs and payload layout are preserved, and peers must match.

Slash presentation adapts the sharp travelling core and contracting afterimage observed in the owner's Orderbringer footage, not its rainbow palette or homing behavior. A narrow white/red blade follows the accepted capsule, with continuous sub-frame amplitude/width variation, elongated recoil filaments and sparse glints. Faint turbulent smoke lingers after the cut and where the left seal catches the beam. Smoke, glints and vibration do not move/extend damage; bright blade light ends with the live interval. Reduced Effects damps decorative flutter, flashes and smoke while retaining warning/strike timing. [Reference analysis](../../research/WOTG_RAID_BENCHMARK.md#f18--orderbringer-cursor-hit-reference-2026-09-20) records observed frames, pinned source and copying boundaries.

### Signature moves (protocol79)

Every third phrase of Acts I-III (`serial % 3 == 0`: the 3rd, 6th, 9th and 12th phrase of a 12-phrase cycle) trades its ordinary notes and its closing crossflow for that Act's **signature move**, played as **four steps a dotted quarter apart** on beats 3.5, 5 and 6.5, the fourth and final step landing on **the next phrase's downbeat** in the crossflow's place (protocol80; protocol79 stepped on four consecutive beats beside the crossflow). Final is unchanged and a signature phrase never takes a pickup. Each step keeps one measured beat of warning, damage capped at the rehearsal 1, takes no player target identity, and is performed by its Act's body ([attack expression](#attack-expression)). Forecast, drawn live footprint and native collision are one capsule list (`CrimsonSignatureMoves` through `CrimsonTechniqueGeometry.Write`); a live footprint never exceeds its forecast.

**Fairness model.** A forecast is visible for one beat (28 ticks) and the player reacts only to what is already shown; steps are now 42-43 ticks apart, so each step leaves a moment between one step's strike and the next forecast. Two mobility profiles are simulated tick by tick in the tests. *Base*: Terraria's run, 0.08 px/tick^2 up to 3 px/tick (about 35 px from rest in one beat), with the field's flight, where gravity is 0.4 (fall at most 10) and holding jump lifts with `Lerp(vy, -12, .18)`. *Endgame*: 0.2 px/tick^2 up to 6 px/tick, typical hardmode wings. `CrimsonFieldPlayer` refills wing time every tick, so everyone flies indefinitely in the field: a held full jump peaks around 130 px, and hovering is never a way to dodge. The Cinder Curtain and Shroud Rope are built for base mobility. Four Hands, like the basic beams, assumes endgame horizontal mobility.

| Act / performer | Move (ID) | Footprint | Live / residue |
|---|---|---|---|
| I / Ember Crown | `CinderCurtain` (17) | Ten 256 px columns of falling fire, top to floor over 3 ticks, except one exactly 1,024 px (four-column) corridor per eligible member; at most six columns burn | 20 / 24 ticks |
| II / Sable Mantle | `ShroudRope` (18) | A five-line staff of 72 px lines (radius 36) crossing the whole field from alternating sides in 2 ticks; even and odd steps use two combs 112 px apart | 12 / 20 ticks |
| III / Thorn Choir | `FourHands` (19) | The field as four 640 px quarters; each note slams two of them with three upright fingers (radius 60) each, 93 px apart | 16 / 24 ticks |

- **Cinder Curtain.** Every eligible member gets a corridor. When the phrase is scheduled the server observes each eligible member's column once and carries the occupied columns as a ten-bit mask in all four notes' `Target` (`Target = (mask, 0)`, an exact integer 1..1023; anything else is rejected, and clients never read it as a place). For each occupied column `o` the corridor is four columns wide and walks **toward the open side, away from the nearer wall** (columns 0-4 walk right, 5-9 walk left), one column per step with one column of forward margin: rightwards `[o-2..o+1] [o-1..o+2] [o..o+3] [o+1..o+4]`, leftwards `[o-1..o+2] [o-2..o+1] [o-3..o] [o-4..o-1]`. The direction follows from where the member stands, so nobody has to wait for a forecast or count phrases (an alternating direction left a member in the trailing edge of their column unable to catch up once note 1 showed it). Next to a wall (`o` = 0, 1, 8, 9) the walk does not fit; it turns toward that wall and each note's corridor is clamped against it, so it slides in and stays: `[0..3]` on all four notes for columns 0 and 1, `[6..9]` for 8 and 9. A note's safe columns are the union of every member's corridor and the remaining columns burn, so a crowd leaves less burning (and the full mask may leave nothing, which is accepted; such a note plays no cue, shake or embers); a lone member sees exactly the single-corridor curtain. Each member's own column is safe for the first three notes inside the field and for all four against a wall. Consecutive corridors of one member overlap by three columns (four against a wall). Columns are exactly 256 px: a burning column beside a safe column keeps exactly half a column, so no corridor is narrowed, and other burning neighbours overlap by 2 px so the wall has no seam. Under base mobility a member who starts running toward the open side when step 0's warning shows survives from any x inside their column (at least 41.8 px from the nearest burning column; 40.2 px at protocol79), and one who waits at the column centre until step 1's warning still survives with at least 155.2 px (74.2 px). Measured from the centre and moving from step 0's warning, the whole phrase is survived with a look-ahead drift of up to 284 px against the walk or 284 px along it (158/284 px). The columns are observed when the phrase is issued, one look-ahead (30 ticks) before step 0's warning as before: a member at rest then, standing or even accelerating the wrong way through the look-ahead, survives from anywhere in the column (at least 4.2 px), and one already running away from the open side at full base speed is lost only from the trailing 92 px of the column (1,104 of 2,844 one-pixel starts over the interior columns, against 2,616 at protocol79, which also lost 1,464 starts that merely accelerated away). A member at a wall just stands still. The strike is felt and heard at the burning column nearest the local player.
- **Shroud Rope.** Odd steps (the final one on the downbeat among them) draw five full-width lines centred 36, 260, 484, 708 and 932 px above the floor, even steps five lines at 148, 372, 596, 820 and 1044 px; every line has radius 36 and keeps it for the whole live window. The combs are built so that no height is ever safe on both steps: the free gap between neighbouring lines of the two combs is 40 px, less than the 42 px body, the lowest line rests on the floor surface and the top line reaches the ceiling. Grounded players keep the jump-rope rhythm: safe on steps 0 and 2, they jump on step 1 and on the final step, which lands on the downbeat; anyone flying higher has to change height every step as well. A comb's live window ends before the next comb is shown. From any safe height a body reaches a safe height for the next step within the one-beat forecast under base flight: the farthest move is 112.5 px (from the ceiling, which needs 24 of the 28 ticks), 72 px from standing on the floor and about 57 px from the middle of any other band. Travel direction alternates left-to-right and right-to-left.
- **Four Hands.** Slammed pairs are `{0,2} {0,3} {1,3} {1,2}`, quarter 0 being the leftmost, rotated by `serial / 3 % 4` so successive signature phrases differ. Each step leaves two safe quarters and consecutive steps always share one, so standing still in the shared quarter survives both. A slammed quarter is three upright fingers (radius 60, 213 px apart, spanning 273 px either side of the quarter centre, 47 px inside its edges) with 93 px gaps between them and 94 px across a quarter boundary; a 47 px strip remains against a field wall, and every gap takes the 20 px body with more than 30 px each side. A body caught inside a slammed quarter has to move at most 71 px to a gap. That is within reach in one forecast beat under endgame mobility (the smallest clearance from a finger over the live window is 12 px from the worst start) but not under base mobility, which is why Four Hands assumes endgame horizontal mobility. The fingers stay upright because any sideways hook would cross the quarter edge or close a gap. They fall over 3 ticks.

The moves draw through the existing soft forecast bands and sound their strikes like every other note; a foretell announces the first and the final step (see [sound effects](#sound-effects)). Their live strike and residue are the field beams' black blood (`ScarletInk`); the residue lies under every forecast and dries early where the move's next step makes that ground safe ([attack expression](#attack-expression)). Protocol79 appends technique IDs 17-19 without renumbering; protocol80 retimes the phrases and shortens the crossflow stream, and matching peers are required (see [network architecture](../../NETWORK_ARCHITECTURE.md#scarlet-phrase-timing-and-crossflow-geometry-protocol80--2026-10-03)). The owner's hand-feel of the corridor, the staff and the finger gaps is unplayed.

## Music: Graceful Ordeal on a 128 BPM grid

Graceful Ordeal is a strict 128 BPM recording: one beat is 22,500 samples (28.125 ticks), a bar is four beats, and song bar n starts at sample 90,000n (bar 0 is the silent lead-in). Measured onsets stay within about2 ms of that grid from the intro to the end. The retired detected beat map (`Score.json`) drifted by up to half a beat in the intro and interlude, and its 43.5-bar loop shifted the meter by two beats on every lap.

Gameplay uses one linear grid from musicStart, which plays song bar 1: beat k is tick musicStart + round(28.125k) (`CrimsonMeter`). The opening is eight bars (900 ticks). Every phrase starts on a bar head and only its key moments sit on bar heads (crossflow and cluster releases, a signature move's final step); ordinary notes use the eighth-note grid between them. A chorus is called on the first bar head after the prior recovery, resolves on the bar head eight beats later and ends one bar after, where the next phrase's pickup crossflow releases. A latched act changes on the next bar head after its cycle completes. The grid never depends on the speakers; the Stack marker arrows and the sky's beat response use the same grid. The previous alternating four-beat helper and detected-beat scheduling are removed.

The client re-sequences whole bars of the unchanged OGG per stage (`CrimsonArrangement`):

| Stage | Song bars |
|---|---|
| Opening + Act I | intro1–8, then lift, riser, pre-drop and the first drop (9–19), then a ten-bar loop alternating the first drop with the matching closing drop |
| Act II | one bar of Act I, the full stop (26), then the bass-led main section (27–30, looping 31–43) |
| Act III | one bar of Act II, the interlude's sweep and valley, the rebuild (56–65), then an eight-bar loop of the closing drop |
| Final | one bar of Act III, the full stop, the last two riser bars swelling in, the pre-drop, then a 25-bar loop that opens on the song's peak (48–55) |

Each later stage's first bar keeps the previous music, so every peer learns a bar-aligned change a bar before anything audible happens. Intra-stage jumps were chosen from measured seam scores (spectral, chroma and level) and blend 14 ms of the real continuation audio. Victory cuts on the next beat into the song's full stop with a short synthetic hall tail; Defeat closes behind a falling low-pass. Normal music returns after the tail. A device stall re-anchors to the authority clock with the same short blend.

Only cut, reordered recording is heard: no time/pitch stretch, re-recording or added instruments. The original WAV remains external/unavailable; attribution records the OGG identity, the stage table and the kuku credit/license exclusion. Numeric seam checks and offline renders do not certify subjective musical continuity; listening and in-game sync remain owner checks.

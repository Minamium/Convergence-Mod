## [Unreleased]

- Make Scarlet Invocation's sound effects easier to hear over its music. Graceful Ordeal now follows the music volume slider the way every other track does: at full volume nothing changes, and lower settings turn it down as much as any other music (at 70% it is about 6 dB quieter than before). The Raid's own sounds were raised a step at a time per kind — strikes and warnings the most, so each note's warning and hit come through the beat, then the crossflow, chorus, act-change and Victory cues and the Down, revive and Ready sounds. The reward weapons' swings, shots, build-ups and cascade hits are 5 dB louder than before (swings, shots and cascade hits about 6 dB under the Raid's strikes, build-ups about 2 dB under its warnings), and their finales and the reliquary's opening now sit just under the crossflow and Victory. Other players' weapon sounds are now all 8 dB quieter than your own (before, only their swings and shots were), so the louder weapons of a full party still leave the Raid's warnings and Stack and Spread calls clear. Every sound keeps the version the owner picked and its balance with the sounds of its own kind. Gameplay, timings and protocol are unchanged.

- Fix the Scarlet Covenant (緋の契約) showing no icon in the inventory, the hotbar or on the ground: an old drawing override still cropped it like the former painting.

- Add the **Scarlet Invocation rewards**: an accepted Victory drops one Scarlet Score Reliquary (緋の聖譜櫃) per member, shared like the Ebon hatboxes, that opens to one of five weapons (20% each) — Sable Scythe (figure-eight swings engrave a five-line staff that Staff Reap and the Final Barline cut across a target), Canticle Organ (bone shards mark enemies; the Hymn of Hands slams a bone hand per mark and the Clasp crushes a fully marked one), Scarlet Baton (conducted black-blood strokes that a Tutti ignites in order, with the Black-Blood River at eight), Ember Censer (crowned censers swing over enemies and pour burning black blood at each apex, with a Grand Pour every fourth) and Bloodink Quill (thrown quills write ink and stick; the Sealed Score stealth strike burns the ink and replays the melody). The five weapons now craft the **Scarlet Covenant** (緋の契約, formerly 紅の盟約) at a Bookcase; the old Grimoire + Silk + Soul of Night recipe is removed, existing Covenants keep working, and the Covenant cannot be shimmered back into the weapons. The Covenant now deals 1500 damage for 10 mana, and Vespera stops attacking while her owner is dead or Down. Items, held weapons, projectiles, the censer and the reliquary's opening show use original pixel art generated by Codex for this set. The set has its own 35 sounds, layered from CC0 recordings and tuned to Graceful Ordeal's E♭: each build step rings the next note of a soft toll ladder for its owner, every release pairs a windup with its strike and ends on an organ-and-bell cadence, the organ's four pipes each have their own shot, and other players hear your swings and shots more quietly, and their sounds never cut yours off. Their levels against Scarlet Invocation's own sounds are set in the loudness entry above. Balance is untuned. Protocol is unchanged.

- Give Scarlet Invocation its own sound set, chosen by the owner from three versions of every scene: sixteen cues built from layered free (CC0) recordings and tuned to Graceful Ordeal's key. Every attack now chimes softly on its warning and lands with a thin glassy strike, the seal crossflow swells for its two beats and breaks open, Stack and Spread each have a call and a distinct success or failure sound, and the act changes, the sacrifice, Victory, going Down, being revived and voting Ready each get their own moment on the music's beat. Sounds no longer cut off abruptly when a chorus call ends. Gameplay, timings and protocol are unchanged.

- Give each Scarlet Invocation Act a signature move on every third phrase (the 3rd, 6th, 9th and 12th of a cycle) in place of that phrase's four basic attacks; the closing seal crossflow stays. Act I's Ember Crown drops a **Cinder Curtain** across the whole field with a four-column (1,024 px) corridor for every player that walks toward the open side (away from the nearer wall), one column per beat; Act II's Sable Mantle cracks a **Shroud Rope**, a five-line staff whose two combs alternate every beat, so grounded players still skip it on the beat and nobody can safely hover; Act III's Thorn Choir slams **Four Hands**, two of four quarters per beat as three fingers with gaps wide enough to step into at endgame speed, and a safe quarter is always shared between beats. The curtain and rope are tuned for base run speed; Four Hands, like the basic beams, assumes endgame mobility. They use the current forecast and strike look and sounds; motions and art come later, and damage stays at the rehearsal 1. Requires matching protocol79 peers.

- Give the Hollow Doll's companion a new arrival sound: a brass key turns and clicks, a short music-box phrase climbs and lands on a high chime over a soft organ breath, replacing the old noise bloom at the same loudness. It plays at the same moment as before; gameplay and protocol are unchanged.

- Fix Requiem of the Hollow Doll endings: in Single Player the raid now reports whether it was won or lost and why (it used to say the end was unknown), and a Defeat ends in the normal death as in multiplayer. A cancel sent while a won score is still settling no longer throws the Victory away. The Central Crush and the final Core check now announce themselves, and the remaining English-only preparation and request messages are translated.

- Redraw Scarlet Invocation's field-beam strikes as a river of black blood: the tracking beam (Act I and Final) and the crossflow stream between the two seals now burn at the rim and melt, with red threads streaming along a black body, a twisting hot core, a blaze for the first ticks and a narrow dried scar afterwards. - Rebuild Scarlet Invocation's music handling: Graceful Ordeal turned out to be a strict 128 BPM recording, so every attack, chorus and act change now sits on its real beat and bar instead of a detected map that drifted by up to half a beat (and slipped two beats every loop). The song is re-sequenced per stage instead of looping: Act I rises from the intro into the first drop, Act II rides the bass-led main section, Act III enters on the interlude's hush, and the sacrifice stops the music, swells the riser and drops the song's peak exactly when the giant becomes hittable.
